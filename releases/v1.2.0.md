## Piano Practice Companion 1.2

### What's new

**A new look**: a **home screen** with today's lesson, the Practice tiles and **your statistics** (day streak, practice time by area this week, accuracy in sightreading, ear training, lesson steps and improvisation, the chords and progressions to work on). **Dark mode** is the default, **Light mode** the other theme, and **My colours** lets you make your own (Appearance, top of the home screen). Right answers flash Perfect, Good or Missed, a good chorus or a finished unit earns a burst of colour (can be turned off).

**Lessons** (new, last button of the main menu): a 46-week jazz piano program for a pianist new to jazz, about 20 to 30 minutes a day. Nine units and a review take you from a first song to the blues, the ii-V-I, the minor ii-V-i, song form and Rhythm Changes, altered dominants, the minor blues, ballad and bossa, and modal playing. Every week has hands together, a song, a few minutes of improvising and ear training.
- The lessons are a **path** of units and week circles; each step has a title and one sentence.
- **Start** opens the step's page: what to do, explained in full sentences, when it is done, why, the **music theory** behind it with scales, voicings and left-hand patterns written on a staff and progressions with their Roman numerals (in the key you choose, or as Roman numerals, and heard with **Listen**), and the books it comes from. The first time a song comes, its page tells its story and its form. **Understood, start** then opens the right screen already set up (song, key, tempo, style, piano pattern, what is checked, muted parts); short exercises are played several times, often a fourth higher each time.
- Steps with a target ("90% right at 80 BPM") are ticked by themselves when you reach it. Nothing is locked; your progress stays on your device.
- Each song is learnt with a left hand that grows in stages: the bass, then the chords, then the song's own pattern, after Jeremy Siskind, Mark Levine and Edward Winn.
- Four units end with a week on a chord substitution once the song is learnt: adding the ii (Avalon), the secondary dominant (Study Blues), the tritone substitution (Honeysuckle Rose), the passing diminished chord (I Got Rhythm), each practised first in a new exercise.
- Intros and endings come back on eight songs: the sheet gets bars before and after the head for them, which the band plays with you, and the step's page shows their chords with Roman numerals and one way to play each, written out. Each unit's improvising brings one tool to build a solo: space, rhythm, motifs, tension and release, a shape over several choruses.
- New songs: Love Me or Leave Me, Black and Tan Fantasy and a Minor Study Blues; new exercises over altered dominants and the minor blues.
- Each improvising step has **Scale ideas for this song**, part by part, one new idea per unit.
- Lick steps open on the lick they teach and say what to listen for in it; dictation steps set how many notes to write; the bossa nova and ballad steps recall their feel, with the bossa rhythm on a staff.
- The back button (and the screen's title) brings you back to the step's page from its exercise.

**Improvisation** (new menu, with a MIDI keyboard)
- **Free Improvisation**: improvise over a song, a random song or endless progressions with the band. Every key you play is written on the sheet and coloured: chord tone, colour tone, approach tone or outside. The page starts again at each chorus.
- The **chord helper** shows the scales for the chord playing; a lesson song's scale ideas sit under the sheet.

**Sightreading**
- **Build your own exercises** (Exercises Sightreading > Build): choose the kind (arpeggios, scales, lines through a progression, approach notes, intervals, rhythm), its settings, the key and how it moves through the keys, and save it under a name as a preset, listed under its category. Six examples ship, one per category; deleted, they come back with Restore all default songs. A lesson step builds its own exercise, with its settings locked.
- **Fewer, better licks**: at most 15 per list, easy to harder (175 in all).
- **Scoring** checks the melody, the chords or both hands; the left hand can play the bass only, the whole chord, the chord on each beat or the band piano's rhythm.
- **Chord Sightreading** voicing drills: shells, two hands, rootless, quartal.
- **Lick Sightreading**: one-chord licks are listed by idea, easy to hard, and follow the chord they lead to.

**Songs and playlists**
- One playlist holds every song of the app: the **App Playlist** (study tunes, OpenEWLD lead sheets, Nottingham folk tunes and the ChoCo chord charts).
- Make your own playlists (**Playlists**, beside New and Import), and delete a playlist with all its songs, only those in no other playlist, or none. A new song is saved into the playlist you choose.
- **Import**: iReal Pro (or MusicXML for lead sheets), **Studio shared links** pasted from Share, or a Studio export; you choose the playlist each import goes into, its name suggested.
- **Export** the playlists you tick, each under the name you want.
- **Edit Song > Manage playlists** puts the song in your playlists or takes it out; once you edit the chart, **Save new song** saves the edits as a new song in the playlist you choose.
- Songs can be deleted for good (Edit Song > Delete song); the app's own songs, and the whole App Playlist, are only hidden: **Restore all default songs** (main menu) brings them back, with the example exercises.
- The **Progressions** filter works for every playlist, with "Any of them" as the default.
- New piano patterns, and every style's menu in one tidy order (bass lines, chords, shells, the Charleston patterns together, voicings).
- Avalon and Tea for Two are now in their real key (F); empty bars show a plain whole rest.

**Practice with the band**
- **Metronome**: a Click strip in the Mixer (off until you turn it on), a bell on each downbeat.
- **Sync** (Mixer): delays the highlight and the scoring when Bluetooth headphones or speakers play late.
- **Start from a chord**: in the chord helper, while the band is stopped; Play starts there, without a count-in.
- **Practice > Keys**: up a fifth, a minor or major third, and **random keys** (all twelve before one comes back).
- **Sheet size**: A− / A+ in the Display menu, from 70% to 175%, for charts and lead sheets.
- **Chord helper**: tap a chord to see it on a keyboard with the scales to improvise with, best first (the chord's scale where it is, its pentatonic, the key's scales, then more), and hear them. It replaces Free Improvisation's Now line; a lesson song's scale ideas sit under the sheet.
- **Substitutions** (beside Practice, on every screen with a band and a song, Free Improvisation included): the band plays chord substitutions, shown in blue on the sheet and described in Roman numerals: the ii added before a long V7, a passing diminished chord, the backdoor ii-V, the turnaround with dominants, the secondary dominant, the tritone substitution, the altered dominant (V7♭9, V7♭9♭13 before a minor chord). Drag them into the order you want: each applies to the chords the ones above it made. Every chorus, from a chosen chorus, every other chorus or at random, also in the downloads. On a lead sheet, none clashes with a long melody note. The infinite progressions add substitutions on their own, at random. The chord helper shows the chord written under a substitute, and the substitute's own scale (Lydian dominant over a tritone substitution).
- **Exercises**: guide-tone exercises over these substitutions, in the exercise builder.
- **Jazz Waltz**: a new style, for every song in 3/4, with Wynton Kelly's waltz comping or a bass on 1 with chords on 2 and 3.
- If a screen ever fails, a message with **Reload** shows instead of a blank page.
- **Download the band**: Export > MIDI, WAV or MP3 of the song as the band plays it (style, pattern, choruses, loop, keys, speed-up, mixer levels).
- Songs in 3/4 no longer get a stray piano chord on the next bar's downbeat, and piano patterns stay on the beat in 3/4 and 6/8.
- The main menu opens faster: each screen and the sound engine load when needed.

**A new name and icon**: the app is now called **Piano Practice Companion**, and its icon and splash screen show the app's music stand.

**Pause and start again** everywhere the band plays: the play button pauses, Play goes on from the same place, and the button beside it plays from the beginning. A new number of choruses goes on from where the band is too, and so does a new style or piano pattern on the endless progressions of Chord Sightreading.

**Delete all my data** (bottom of the main menu) erases everything the app keeps on the device, after a second confirmation.

### How to get it

| Device | What to do |
|---|---|
| Computer, tablet or phone | open the [web demo](https://dylan-sam.github.io/piano-practice-companion/) in a browser: nothing to install |
| Android phone (Android 7.0 or newer) | download **piano-practice-companion-debug.apk** below, open it, allow the install when Android asks, then tap **Install** |

Already have 1.1 on Android? Install this APK over it; your songs and settings are kept. If Android refuses, uninstall the old version first (use **Export → All 3** before, as uninstalling erases your saved songs).
